# Customer chat with Lena (Juniper Salon), verbatim

About 25 of 50 turns used (the chat page's own counter is authoritative). The question "In that situation, what would you have wanted to happen for the second client?" was sent twice by mistake; both answers are included.

**Lena:** Hi, I run a small neighborhood salon. Last-minute cancellations leave gaps in our day, and I spend a lot of time texting people who asked for an earlier appointment. I’d like a simple way to handle that without all the back-and-forth.

**Me:** That sounds like a lot of texting. Could you walk me through the last time a cancellation happened, starting from when the client cancelled?

**Lena:** Usually Lena or Carla notices the cancellation in Square, then checks the Google Sheet for people who might fit. We text likely matches from the salon phone, wait for replies, and try to remember who has been contacted. If nobody takes it, the chair just stays empty.

**Me:** You mentioned checking the Google Sheet for "likely matches." How do you decide who fits a cancelled slot, and who do you text first?

**Lena:** We look for the same service, a time they can actually make, and their preferred stylist if they listed one. If several people fit, we usually start with whoever joined the waitlist earliest.

**Me:** After you text the first person on that list, what usually happens next?

**Lena:** We wait for a reply, but there isn’t a consistent process for how long. If they decline or don’t answer, we try the next person, assuming someone remembers to do it.

**Me:** When a cancellation comes in, how much time do you usually have before that appointment would have started?

**Lena:** It varies, but many are inside 48 hours. Same-day openings are the hardest because we have very little time to fill them.

**Me:** You said same-day openings are the hardest. What's the latest you'd still try to fill one?

**Lena:** We’ll try right up until the appointment time if someone might reasonably get there, but realistically the chances drop a lot in the last hour.

**Me:** When someone says yes, what do you do next?

**Lena:** We put them on the calendar and tell the other people not to come in. It can get messy if replies arrive close together, since different staff may be handling texts.

**Me:** You mentioned telling the other people not to come in. Do you ever text more than one person about the same opening at the same time?

**Lena:** Yes, sometimes. I think it gives us the best chance of filling the opening quickly, but it can create competing acceptances.

**Me:** Can you tell me about a time two people said yes to the same opening? What happened?

**Lena:** Last month, two clients replied yes to a Saturday haircut after both got the group text. Different staff members answered them, and both thought they were booked. One client arrived angry, and we had to turn her away.

**Me:** In that situation, what would you have wanted to happen for the second client?

**Lena:** They should have been told immediately that the slot was already taken, with a polite offer to keep them on the waitlist for another opening.

**Me:** In that situation, what would you have wanted to happen for the second client?

**Lena:** They should have gotten a quick message saying the slot was already taken, and we could have kept them on the waitlist for another opening.

**Me:** At the moment a slot opens up, what do you need to see, and what do you want to decide yourself?

**Lena:** I need to enter the service, stylist, date, and time. I want the system to show me the eligible waitlist people and move through them without me having to manage every text.

**Me:** While the system is working through that list, what do you want to be able to see?

**Lena:** I want to see who currently has the offer, who declined or timed out, who is still eligible, and whether the opening was filled. I also need to know if the process stopped or someone canceled.

**Me:** You mentioned wanting to know if someone canceled. Who do you mean, and what should happen when that happens?

**Lena:** I mean the client who accepted, or the stylist becoming unavailable. Staff should be able to cancel the offer or booking process, and the client should be told it’s no longer available.

**Me:** When a client does reply to an offer, how long does it usually take?

**Lena:** For a same-day opening, I’d give them 15 minutes. If they don’t answer by then, it should move to the next person automatically.

**Me:** And for an opening that's a day or two away, how long would you give someone to reply?

**Lena:** We don’t have a set rule for those. It depends on how desirable the time is and how busy we are.

**Me:** How do clients get onto the waitlist, and what did they agree to about being contacted?

**Lena:** They’re added by us when they ask for an earlier or different appointment. We record their service, stylist preference, phone number, and general availability, and they agree that we may text them about openings.

**Me:** In a typical week, how many openings come up, and how many do you manage to fill today?

**Lena:** Probably 8 to 12 cancellations within 48 hours each week. We refill around three of those, usually.

**Me:** If this worked well, what would be different a month from now, and how would you know?

**Lena:** I’d want at least half of those last-minute cancellations refilled without us repeatedly checking on them. I’d know from the appointment results and whether we had to chase the process manually.

**Me:** Let me check I've got this right. When a cancellation comes in, you enter the service, stylist, date, and time. The system shows the eligible waitlist people (same service, a time they can make, their preferred stylist, earliest on the list first) and offers the slot, giving same-day clients 15 minutes before it moves to the next person automatically. For slots a day or two away, staff decide how long to wait. The first person to accept gets the slot, everyone else gets a quick message that it's taken and stays on the waitlist, and staff can cancel at any time. You want to see who has the offer, who declined or timed out, who's still eligible, and whether the opening was filled. Success is refilling at least half of the 8 to 12 weekly last-minute cancellations without chasing it by hand. What did I get wrong or miss?

**Lena:** That’s mostly right. I’d also need clear notification to the client and front desk when it’s filled, and the client shouldn’t need an account.

**Me:** What's the one thing that, if it didn't work, would make this not worth it for you?

**Lena:** If it allowed two people to claim the same opening, it wouldn’t be worth it. Avoiding that confusion matters more than sending messages as fast as possible.

**Me:** Is there anything I haven't asked that I should have?

**Lena:** I’m not sure. The important part is that it keeps moving to the next person without creating another double-booking.

## Follow-up questions asked after the first pass (about 3 minutes, still inside the turn limit)

The four questions are shown as I drafted them for the chat window. Lena's answers are copied exactly.

**Me:** Once someone accepts, who should put them on the calendar: the system, or your staff?

**Lena:** Our staff should do it. The prototype doesn’t need to update Square; we’ll update the real calendar ourselves.

**Me:** What does a client need to know in the first message to say yes or no?

**Lena:** They need the service, stylist, date, time, and a clear deadline for replying. They should be able to accept or decline from their phone without creating an account.

**Me:** If nobody on the list takes the opening, what should happen then?

**Lena:** It should show as unfilled and tell the front desk that the process is finished. We may try other normal outreach ourselves, but the system shouldn’t keep going indefinitely.

**Me:** Are there times of day when clients shouldn’t be texted?

**Lena:** Yes, I’d avoid very early mornings and late evenings. We don’t have a precise cutoff, but normal daytime and early evening is best.

## One more point from Lena, sent after the build had started
